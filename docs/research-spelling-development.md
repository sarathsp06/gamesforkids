# Research Brief: How Children 4–8 Learn to Spell & Read Words

## Developmental stages of spelling
- **Gentry (1982)** — building on Charles Read's work — identifies 5 stages: *precommunicative* (random letters), *semiphonetic* (partial sound mapping, e.g. KT for cat), *phonetic* (full sound mapping, KAT for cat), *transitional* (sound-correct + visual conventions, e.g. HIGHKED for hiked), *correct/conventional*. Stages overlap; one writing sample can show several. [Reading Rockets / Gentry](https://www.readingrockets.org/topics/spelling-and-word-study/articles/invented-spelling-and-spelling-development)
- **Ehri's 4 phases of word reading** mirror this: *pre-alphabetic* → *partial alphabetic* → *full alphabetic* → *consolidated alphabetic* (chunks like -ING, -IGHT stored as units). Progress = richer letter↔sound connections, not memorized whole words. [Ehri, American Educator 2023](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1394529.pdf)
- A 4–8-year-old is typically moving from semiphonetic → phonetic → transitional; conventional accuracy is NOT the expected norm in this age band.

## Invented spelling is a good sign, not an error
- **Read (1971) / Gentry**: invented spellings are systematic, rule-governed hypotheses about sound–letter mapping — evidence of analysis, not sloppiness. [Reading Rockets](https://www.readingrockets.org/topics/spelling-and-word-study/articles/invented-spelling-and-spelling-development)
- **Ouellette & Sénéchal (2017, Developmental Psychology)**: kindergarteners' invented-spelling sophistication predicted Grade 1 reading beyond letter knowledge and phonological awareness; guided invented spelling *caused* reading gains in their 2008 training study. [EdWeek summary](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/invented-spelling-leads-to-better-reading-study-says/2017/05), [Ouellette & Sénéchal 2008, Child Development](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2008.01166.x)
- **Sénéchal, Ouellette & Nguyen (2023)** integrative review confirms descriptive, correlational, and causal evidence that invented spelling supports literacy — when paired with feedback that nudges toward conventional forms. [Handbook on the Science of Early Literacy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385038675)

## Phonological awareness & letter-sound mapping
- Phonemic awareness (segmenting/blending sounds) is the engine of both reading and spelling; spelling attempts are a window into it. **Ehri's "orthographic mapping"**: sight words are learned by bonding graphemes to phonemes, not by visual rote. [Ehri 2014, Scientific Studies of Reading](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1027413)
- **Treiman & Kessler**: children exploit letter *names* (e.g. R for "are", KAM for came) and legal letter patterns; these errors are phonologically lawful. [Treiman & Kessler 2003](https://ga.dyslexiaida.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/27/2022/11/Treiman_Kessler_2003_role_of_letter_names.pdf)
- **Bourassa & Treiman**: misspellings preserving the "phonological skeleton" (PAK for pack) differ qualitatively from skeleton-violating ones (PK, BAK-less random strings). [Linguistic pattern analysis, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3517694)

## Graded scoring already exists in research — don't invent from scratch
- **Tangel & Blachman (1992) Developmental Spelling Test**: scores each attempt 0–6 by phonological completeness (random letter < first sound < skeleton < full phonetic < near-conventional < correct). Widely used metric. [End-of-K spelling outcomes, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502407)
- **Caravolas, Hulme & Snowling (2001)**: per-grapheme 0–4 proximity scoring; phonologically-sensitive partial-credit scores predict later spelling better than binary right/wrong. [Scoring methods comparison, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6364987)

## Dutch vs English orthography
- **Seymour, Aro & Erskine (2003)**: foundational literacy takes ~1 year in transparent orthographies vs 2.5+ years in English, the deepest European orthography. [Early Reading Development in European Orthographies](https://sites.pitt.edu/~perfetti/PDF/Seymour.pdf)
- Dutch is relatively transparent; word-reading fluency is acquired faster in Dutch than English, though skills correlate highly across the two. [Acquiring reading in Dutch and English, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2820218)
- Consequence: Dutch 6-year-olds hit near-ceiling decoding earlier → Dutch word lists can advance in length/complexity faster; English lists need a longer phonetic-stage runway and explicit handling of irregular words (said, one, eight).
- Dutch pain points are different: vowel length doubling (maan/man, boom/bom), ei/ij, au/ou, d/t finals (devoicing) — phonologically identical, orthographically conventional. These are *transitional*-stage issues, not phonological failures.

## Error type → developmental meaning → game response
| Error type (example for CAT) | Developmental meaning | Game should |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Random/unrelated letters (MXP) | Precommunicative; no sound mapping yet | 0 credit; model the word, replay its sounds; drop to easier level |
| Correct first sound only (K, KT) | Semiphonetic — real progress at age 4–5 | Partial credit (~30–50%); praise the captured sounds, stretch the word aloud |
| Full phonetic match (KAT; Dutch: SGOOL for school) | Phonetic stage — strong phonemic awareness; best predictor of reading | High credit (~75–90%); "You heard every sound!" then show conventional form once, no penalty vibe |
| Phonologically close, one sound off (KET, CAD) | Partial segmentation; voicing/vowel confusion is typical | Medium credit (~50–70%); highlight the one differing sound |
| Skeleton preserved, convention missed (PAK/pack; Dutch: MAN/maan, HONT/hond) | Transitional — sounds mastered, orthographic rules pending | Good credit (~70–85%); teach the specific convention (ck, vowel doubling, d/t) |
| Letter-name spelling (R for are, KAM for came) | Lawful letter-name strategy (Treiman) | Treat as phonetic-stage credit, not random error |
| Semantically wrong word spelled fine (MAN for CAT) | Comprehension/attention miss, not spelling | 0–low credit regardless of spelling quality — different skill axis |
| Transposition/near-miss keyboard slip (CTA) | Motor/typing, not linguistic | Light penalty; offer instant retry |

## Implications for our games
1. Score by **phonological distance, not edit distance**: KAT ≫ CTA ≫ MXP for CAT, even though edit distance says otherwise. Use grapheme-to-phoneme comparison per language (Tangel–Blachman / Caravolas style 0–N rubric).
2. **Never mark phonetic spellings as simply "wrong"** for ages 4–6; show the conventional form as a bonus ("Books spell it C-A-T") after crediting the sounds heard.
3. **Separate skill axes**: sound mapping (credit generously), orthographic convention (teach gently from ~6–7), meaning/word choice (binary).
4. **Level by Ehri phase**, not age alone: partial-alphabetic → 2–3 phoneme CVC words; full-alphabetic → blends/digraphs; consolidated → chunks (-ight, Dutch -ng, doubled vowels).
5. **Language-specific curves**: Dutch levels can ramp faster and focus convention-stage drills on vowel doubling, ei/ij, d/t; English needs a longer phonetic runway plus a separate irregular-word (sight word) track.
6. **Arithmetic parallel holds**: credit the process (off-by-one, right operation) over binary correctness — same partial-credit philosophy.
